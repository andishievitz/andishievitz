New-Client Position Extractor

The friction: when a new client moves assets to the firm, we first have to confirm we can actually hold every position they own. That meant hours with the ACATS (transfer) team combing through long statements from the client's old firm, one line at a time: page headers, account summaries, wrapped descriptions, bonds priced as a percent of par, trade activity, and fine print.

The agent: reads every page, extracts each holding by account, reconciles the totals so nothing is missed, and checks each position against the firm's security master and transfer rules. It clears everything that can move in kind and hands the ACATS team a short action list of the few positions that actually need verification.

Try it

  1. Pick a new client and scroll through their raw statement.
  2. Click Run agent. See how short the action list is compared to the statement.
  3. Edit the statement (try changing a price or adding a line) and run it again.

Pitch Book Checker

The friction: each reporting period, client review decks were rolled forward from the last quarter. Any figure that didn't get refreshed (an old date, last quarter's return, allocations that no longer add up) could end up in front of a client.

The agent: compares every figure on the slide to the reporting system for the current period, flags stale or mismatched numbers, explains why each one is wrong, and suggests the fix.

Try it

  1. Choose a client review deck and click Check slide.
  2. Click any number on the slide to edit it, then check again.
  3. Use Apply suggested fixes to correct everything at once.

Client Spending Summarizer

The friction: before a fund-raising conversation, a Client Service Associate needs to know what a client actually spends. For a wealthy family that can mean 25 accounts (checking, credit cards, LLCs, kids' accounts, IRAs, 529s, a donor-advised fund, a credit line) and hundreds of cryptic transactions every quarter. Money moving between their own accounts and card payoffs makes the outflows look several times bigger than real spending.

The agent: consolidates every account, strips out internal transfers, card payments, trades, and capital calls, avoids double counts, categorizes what's left from raw merchant text, and checks liquidity against upcoming capital calls, then writes the CSA a one-page brief.

Try it

  1. Pick a client and look at the account list and raw ledger.
  2. Filter the ledger by account to see how messy each one is.
  3. Click Summarize to see gross outflows reduced to true spending.

Capital Call & Distribution Tracker

The friction: private equity, credit, and real estate funds call capital and pay distributions on their own schedules. Updates were scattered across fund calls and documents, and figuring out where each call would be funded from often happened at the last minute.

The tool: organized the way the team works: by fund, with every client in that fund underneath. Each fund shows its next call or distribution and the GP notice status. Each client row shows their share of the call, their status, and the CSA's funding plan: where the FA may want to take funds from, and whether funds need to be raised. Every fund's call history is one click away. This recreation covers 8 funds, 8 clients, and $113.5M in mock commitments.

Try it

  1. Pick a client's Fund from account. The tracker warns you if that account is short for their share of the call.
  2. Mark Raise funds? and leave a note for the FA.
  3. Click Call history on any fund to see every past call and distribution.
